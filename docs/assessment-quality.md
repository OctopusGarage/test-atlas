# Assessment and source quality

Source review date: **2026-11-13**. This report covers the current 25 assessments: **401 items and 117 dimensions**, with Chinese and English presentations. It distinguishes useful reflection prompts, recognizable psychological concepts, and evidence supporting a particular measurement interpretation. These are different achievements.

**The catalog is not a collection of 25 validated psychological tests.** One bank reproduces a public-domain inventory; 17 are original reflection banks; seven are playful original activities. The 18 non-play banks do not collectively meet the evidential requirements for a scientific assessment readout. Original wording, a plausible theoretical reference, correct scoring, and a polished report cannot establish reliability, validity, diagnosis, career suitability, or relationship compatibility.

## Provenance, permission and adaptation

**Big Five is the strongest provenance case, not an unrestricted validity claim.** Its 50 items retain the [IPIP five-factor key](https://ipip.ori.org/newBigFive5broadKey.htm), including 24 reverse-keyed items. English preserves source content with normalized sentence grammar; Chinese is a project translation without independent cross-cultural validation. [IPIP permits public-domain reuse and adaptation](https://ipip.ori.org/newPermission.htm); permission does not validate edits or translations. The [IPIP-50 explanation](https://ipip.ori.org/new_ipip-50-item-scale.htm) supplies inventory context. Ten items per broad factor do not establish facet measurement. Emotional stability must retain its displayed direction; 0–100 scores are response-range transformations, not population percentiles.

**Attachment, dark themes and Enneagram are original alternatives.** The [ECR-R author's documentation](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/~rcfraley/measures/ecrr.htm) describes 36 items, 18 each for anxiety and avoidance, with seven-point responding; our 12 items are neither ECR-R nor ECR-RS. Research access should not be interpreted as blanket commercial permission. The [original SD3 development paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/1073191113514105) concerns a 27-item measure and empirical development across four studies, unlike our 18 original questions. The [RHETI provider](https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/rheti/) describes 144 paired statements; its validation claims belong to that product, not our 27-item narrative bank, and are not independently endorsed here.

**Strengths coverage is not VIA equivalence.** Our 72 original items cover 24 concepts. [VIA offers multiple assessment forms](https://www.viacharacter.org/researchers/assessments), including a 72-item form; coincident item counts do not make ours that instrument. Official material has [permission requirements](https://www.viacharacter.org/faq/permissions-and-guidelines-for-use-and-interpretation) and [terms of service](https://www.viacharacter.org/terms-of-service). The [public-domain IPIP-VIA alternative](https://ipip.ori.org/newVIAKey.htm) is a separate bank and is not used here.

**Six-factor content is not HEXACO-PI-R.** [The official inventory](https://hexaco.org/hexaco-inventory) provides 60- and 100-item forms under nonprofit academic research conditions and restricts public website/app administration. This project has no license for that official instrument and uses original items instead. Three prompts per domain do not cover its 24 facets; emotionality is not interchangeable with Big Five neuroticism. The [preliminary IPIP analog](https://ipip.ori.org/newHEXACO_PI_key.htm) is another distinct public-domain option, not used here.

## Nine topic-specific comparisons

**Emotional interaction:** the [MSCEIT v2 documentation](https://www.eiconsortium.org/measures/msceit.html) describes 141 ability-test items and four branches. Our nine self-descriptions cannot establish demonstrated ability or an EQ. Noticing triggers, knowing calming activities and checking another person's feelings are plausible habits, but their three-factor structure is untested.

**Receiving care:** the five topics resemble [Chapman's framework](https://5lovelanguages.com/help/love-language), without reproducing its official quiz. The [2024 research review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/09637214231217663) found weak support for a primary language, exactly five languages, and matching as a route to satisfaction. Current receiving-care wording corrects the earlier mismatch between questions about feeling cared for and descriptions about expressing care. Differences are conversation prompts, not compatibility percentages.

**Romantic rhythm:** the [LAS short-form development paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0265407598152001) investigated six attitudes and both four- and three-item-per-attitude forms, shortening original seven-item subscales. Our three themes are neither LAS nor its short form. Future planning, everyday imagining and rituals may overlap without forming one demonstrated factor.

**Conflict:** the [TKI creator explains 30 forced-choice pairs](https://kilmanndiagnostics.com/assessments/tki-faqs/) designed to compare five modes while balancing social desirability. Our 15 independent agreement statements use a different method. Cooling off is not necessarily avoidance; firm advocacy is not necessarily competing. Similar labels cannot make the scores interchangeable.

**Career interests:** [O*NET's Interest Profiler](https://www.onetcenter.org/IP.html) measures six RIASEC areas with a 30-item Mini-IP or 60-item Short Form. Our 18 original items sample a narrower range of activities. Familiarity, opportunity and confidence can affect endorsement alongside interest. Linked occupational examples support exploration, not validated matching probabilities or proof of ability.

**Work values:** [O*NET's Locator interpretation](https://www.onetcenter.org/dl_files/WIL_zips/WIL-SR-deskp.pdf) describes six values; [CareerOneStop's matcher](https://cloudfront.careeronestop.org/Toolkit/Careers/work-values-matcher-help.aspx) uses a 20-statement card sort. Our five agreement scales do not reproduce that procedure. An absolute endorsement score cannot establish that a value ranks first, especially when all priorities can be endorsed strongly.

**Learning:** the [original MSLQ manual](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED338122) describes 31 motivation items plus 50 strategy/resource-management items for a college course. Our nine general prompts are not MSLQ. Retrieval practice, feedback and study conditions are reasonable reflection topics, but reported use does not prove effective implementation, learning outcomes or a fixed learner type.

**Procrastination:** [Steel's original study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191886910000930) investigated subtype claims and derived the Pure Procrastination Scale; the [author-hosted paper](https://procrastinus.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/arousal-avoidant-and-decisional-procrastinators-do-they-exist.pdf) documents selection of 12 items. Our nine prompts concern aversion, standards pressure and choice overload. These are possible accompanying conditions, not established causes or stable procrastinator types.

**Decision approaches:** [Scott and Bruce's development study](https://doi.org/10.1177/0013164495055005017) investigated rational, intuitive, dependent, avoidant and spontaneous styles. The [SJDM instrument inventory](https://sjdm.org/dmidi/General_Decision_Making_Style.html) documents 25 items across those five scales. Our four themes replace several constructs with risk caution and small experiments. Intuitive confidence does not demonstrate expertise or accuracy.

## What the current evaluation establishes

The initial **model-assisted editorial review** examined 401 Chinese/English item pairs and 117 dimension low/high interpretation pairs. Its rubric considered semantic alignment, construct fit, response-demand clarity, loaded comparisons, and whether reports respected score direction and neutral answers. It identified 25 item flags: 11 semantic and 14 design concerns. This was qualitative assistance, not a blinded panel, calibrated scientific judge, human expert certification or validation study; flag counts are review observations, not error-rate estimates.

Revisions addressed the 11 semantic flags, a strengths willingness-versus-behavior mismatch, and one loaded teamwork comparison. Report revisions cover 18 specific narrative cases, adjust actions to score context, and correct receiving-care terminology. These changes improve coherence; they do not settle the remaining construct concerns below. A model finding no additional issue is not evidence that an item is valid.

Separate **601 deterministic rule tests** cover all 25 banks in both languages, nine response profiles, an independent item-position scoring oracle, reverse keys, complement/reordering invariants, ties, localization identity and social reports. They are automated software checks, **not AI-judge evaluations**. Additional report checks cover 250 report profiles and 1,170 dimension-band comparisons. These counts describe scenarios checked, not validated people, reliability estimates or scientific accuracy scores.

The post-revision editorial check regenerated those 250 profiles and 1,170 band comparisons with no mismatches, and independently reread the changed wording. This checks consistency with the declared rules; it does not certify every remaining interpretation as scientifically supported.

## Application verification

The 2026-11-13 verification passed TypeScript checking, content fingerprint checking, **669 unit/rule tests**, and **149 browser cases** across the complete regression run and focused follow-up checks. `pnpm test:eval` runs a 611-test subset covering the scoring matrix and content regressions. Browser coverage includes Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, questionnaire completion, drafts, deletion, sharing and QR decoding, invitation transitions, consent, translations and historical versions. A further 106 browser cases are deliberately skipped: the complete 25-topic journeys and report scans run in Chromium, and service-worker lifecycle checks run in Chromium; shared journeys and mobile navigation run on all three engines. These skips are not successful executions.

Automated accessibility checks cover the main pages in both languages on all three engines and all 25 reports in both languages in Chromium. Layout checks include 320-pixel reports and 1280-pixel desktop pages. Repairs include text contrast, very small text, accessible card names and chart semantics. Short mobile screens keep question actions visible. A history-filter overflow exposed by Linux browser font metrics was reproduced with enlarged control text and repaired; the regression runs on all three engines. A premature catalog count assertion was replaced with a condition-based UI assertion and repeated five times per engine. Browser simulation is not a physical-device installation test, a human usability study or an accessibility certification.

A local production-build Lighthouse 13.6.0 mobile-emulation run scored 100 for performance, accessibility, best practices and SEO, with 0.8-second LCP, 0 milliseconds total blocking time and zero measured layout shift. This is one laboratory run on this machine, not field Core Web Vitals or a promise about mobile networks. The production build precaches approximately 942 KiB, including historical questionnaire definitions needed to reopen saved reports. Dependency audit reports one low-severity development dependency issue and no high/critical issues at this review date.

## Remaining item-design review agenda

These 13 residual flags are plausible risks requiring expert adjudication and respondent interviews, **not established measurement failures**. Wording repairs alone cannot determine whether a construct should be split, reassigned or measured differently.

| Bank / item | Residual concern |
|---|---|
| `enneagram` / `D6Q3` | Checking trust against observed behavior may reflect ordinary calibrated trust rather than security motivation. |
| `enneagram` / `D8Q3` | Resistance versus passive waiting may favor action and confound autonomy with activity. |
| `strengths` / `D9Q3` | Conditioning participation on already having energy may measure willingness rather than experienced zest; modality wording was repaired. |
| `strengths` / `D13Q1` | Completing agreed work may reflect conscientiousness or perseverance without teamwork-specific interaction. |
| `strengths` / `D16Q3` | Considering an offender's context while maintaining boundaries joins multiple demands. |
| `love-expression` / `D3Q1` | Remembering something mentioned need not involve a gift or material token. |

| `boundaries` / `D2Q3` | A normative belief about device-access consent differs from enacted boundary behavior. |

## Evidential limits and next steps

No documented expert-review sample, validation sample size, omega reliability, exploratory/confirmatory factor analysis, retest study, bilingual measurement invariance or population norms establishes the original banks' proposed interpretations. Three items per dimension and positive-only wording are **not automatically invalid**; they require evidence and can limit coverage or increase response-style sensitivity. Equally, adding reverse wording or citing a theory would not supply that evidence. With three five-option items, a dimension has only 13 possible raw totals, despite a 0–100 display. Editorial bands are not clinical thresholds, and cross-dimension ranks can reflect different endorsement difficulty.

For professional assessment use, define intended interpretations and populations, commission qualified content review and cognitive interviews, establish permitted source/translation use, and design empirical studies appropriate to those claims. The [Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing](https://www.testingstandards.net/uploads/7/6/6/4/76643089/standards_2014edition.pdf) concern evidence for specified interpretations and uses; [ITC adaptation guidance](https://www.intestcom.org/page/14) makes language and cultural adaptation a substantive task. Do not copy copyrighted comparator questions without appropriate permission. For present use, retain explicit original/play labels, contextual questions, non-diagnostic limitations and optional practical experiments. The application remains a local rule-based PWA; this review introduces no runtime AI service or AI-generated personal assessment.
